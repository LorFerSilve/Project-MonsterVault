# Target Audience and Platform Strategy

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Owning phase:** GDS-1  
> **Authority:** Product-level audience and platform targeting

## 1. Primary Audience

MonsterVault is primarily designed for **Roblox players aged approximately 9–15** who enjoy:

- collecting and completing sets;
- visible progression and upgrades;
- discovering rare or surprising variants;
- short-to-medium gameplay loops with clear next goals;
- playing alongside friends without requiring constant coordination;
- social status, showcasing, competition, and shared events;
- games that are easy to understand immediately but support long-term mastery and collection goals.

The experience must not assume advanced gaming literacy, complex economic knowledge, high reading tolerance, or precision input.

## 2. Secondary Audience

A secondary audience consists of **older teens and young adults, approximately 16–24+**, especially players who enjoy:

- collection completion;
- optimization and progression planning;
- rarity hunting;
- event participation;
- trading/value speculation when trading is eventually enabled;
- social flexing through difficult-to-acquire creatures and vault presentation.

The design should therefore avoid feeling exclusively childish even while remaining readable and appropriate for younger players.

## 3. Younger Players

Players aged 5–8 are **not the primary design target**.

The game should still avoid unnecessary complexity or unsuitable content that would make broad platform access impossible, but GDS-1 does not require every progression layer to be optimized for independent use by very young players.

Exact Roblox content-maturity and age-access constraints are owned by GDS-15.

## 4. Global Audience

MonsterVault is intended for an international Roblox audience.

Product-level consequences:

- core actions should be understandable with limited text;
- essential information should use icons, spatial cues, concise labels, and repeated visual language;
- humor or progression logic must not depend on culture-specific references;
- the core loop must remain understandable before localization is perfect;
- no progression-critical mechanic may depend on unrestricted free-form chat.

Localization strategy and UI implementation details are owned by later presentation/technical phases.

## 5. Device Priority

MonsterVault uses a **mobile-first interaction constraint with cross-platform gameplay parity**.

This means:

1. touch usability is a first-class design constraint;
2. desktop may offer more precise camera/input convenience but not exclusive core capabilities;
3. tablet is treated as part of the touch target;
4. console support is required for the core experience unless a later technical/platform constraint is formally accepted;
5. no baseline mechanic may require keyboard-only shortcuts, tiny UI targets, high-precision aiming, or mouse-specific interaction.

Detailed input mappings are owned by GDS-3 and presentation/device behavior by GDS-14.

## 6. Social Independence

MonsterVault is multiplayer-first, but the **core progression loop must remain playable without voice chat, free-form text chat, or a premade friend group**.

Social systems should improve the experience through:

- shared events;
- visible collections/status;
- optional cooperation;
- bounded competition;
- parties/friend play;
- later safe trading.

They must not make a solo-joining player feel that meaningful progression is unavailable.

## 7. Accessibility-Level Product Requirement

At product level, the game must avoid relying on a single sensory or device channel for critical information.

Later specifications must preserve at minimum:

- color-independent critical rarity/state cues;
- no audio-only gameplay instructions;
- readable touch targets;
- clear next-goal presentation;
- alternatives to precision-heavy interaction where the mechanic is progression-critical.

Detailed accessibility behavior is owned by GDS-14.

## 8. Tone Implications

The intended product tone is:

- colorful;
- energetic;
- slightly mysterious;
- aspirational;
- collectible/status-driven;
- exciting rather than threatening;
- playful rather than cynical or grim.

MonsterVault is not positioned as horror, realistic combat, or a mature social hangout.

## 9. Audience Success Principle

A successful MonsterVault player does not need to understand every underlying system. The player should be able to answer three questions quickly:

1. **What can I do now?**
2. **What can I get next?**
3. **Why would that be exciting or valuable to me?**

If the product requires extensive explanation before those answers are clear, the audience contract is not being met.
