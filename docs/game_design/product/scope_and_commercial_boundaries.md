# Scope and Commercial Boundaries

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Owning phase:** GDS-1  
> **Authority:** High-level scope discipline, launch priorities, commercial intensity, and explicit non-goals

## 1. Scope Philosophy

MonsterVault should become deep through **interconnected collection/progression systems and content variety**, not through unrelated feature accumulation.

The project favors a small number of strong, reusable systems over many shallow mechanics.

Every major feature should materially strengthen at least one of:

- creature discovery/capture;
- collection desire;
- visible vault/progression;
- exploration/unlocks;
- social/server moments;
- long-term return motivation;
- fair commercial sustainability.

## 2. Launch-Critical Product Capabilities

The eventual launch product must contain enough content and systems to sustain the core promise, including:

- a complete capture/secure/return loop;
- persistent creature collection;
- a functional visible vault/base;
- meaningful rarity/variant hunting;
- progression/unlocks;
- more than one meaningful collection aspiration;
- multiplayer presence with at least one strong shared/social mechanic;
- robust onboarding and feedback;
- reliable persistence;
- enough content variety that the product does not feel exhausted after a single short session.

Exact creature counts, biome counts, mutation counts, upgrade counts, event counts, and vertical-slice contents are **not locked by GDS-1**. They are derived later from subsystem design, production capacity, playtesting, and TA-17 implementation planning.

## 3. Trading Position

Trading is strategically desirable for long-term collection value and community depth, but it is **not a launch-critical dependency**.

Trading should ship only when:

- creature identity/ownership semantics are mature;
- persistence is reliable;
- value transfer can be made atomic and auditable;
- duplication/scam risks are acceptably controlled;
- the economy has enough scarcity/value differentiation to make trading meaningful.

GDS-12 owns the exact trading design.

## 4. Monetization Intensity

MonsterVault targets a **moderate, visible-but-non-coercive monetization model**.

The product should be commercially sustainable without making payment feel mandatory for normal collection progress.

Permitted high-level monetization directions include:

- cosmetics and visual status;
- vault themes/presentation;
- storage or capacity convenience where the free path remains viable;
- bounded convenience/acceleration;
- starter-value offers;
- server-wide boosts or events that create shared value;
- future passes/subscriptions only if later evidence supports them.

Product-level prohibited directions include:

- selling the strongest or rarest long-term collectible as the normal primary acquisition path;
- permanent paid advantages that make free-earned progress strategically irrelevant;
- intentionally painful progression designed mainly to sell relief;
- irreversible loss or punishment for refusing to spend;
- misleading purchase presentation.

GDS-13 owns exact products, prices, purchase timing, and fairness rules.

## 5. Live-Operations Position

MonsterVault is intended to support ongoing content additions.

The product should be designed so that new:

- creatures;
- variants/mutations;
- biomes;
- events;
- cosmetics;
- progression targets

can extend the experience without replacing the core loop.

A **roughly weekly small-update/live-event capability** is a desirable operational target, but GDS-1 does not create a promise that every calendar week must contain a major content release. Exact cadence is owned by GDS-11/GDS-16 and must reflect actual production capacity.

## 6. Long-Term Progression Horizon

The product should support meaningful goals across **weeks to months**, not only one-session completion.

Long-term depth should primarily come from:

- collection completion;
- rare variants;
- biome/progression unlocks;
- vault development;
- rotating/live opportunities;
- social status;
- later trading/value discovery.

GDS-1 does not require infinite stat scaling or mandatory prestige resets.

## 7. Explicit Baseline Non-Goals

The following are not baseline product requirements:

- a narrative campaign;
- systemic direct-combat PvP;
- ranked competitive ladders;
- clans/guild warfare;
- large-scale open-world simulation;
- complex crafting trees;
- breeding/fusion systems;
- full user-generated creature creation;
- MMO-scale cross-server persistent worlds;
- unrestricted theft of secured persistent creatures;
- photorealistic art direction;
- a general-purpose reusable Roblox framework as the primary deliverable.

A later phase may propose one of these, but it must justify the addition against the product pillars and receive explicit authority before Design Complete.

## 8. Small-Team Feasibility Constraint

MonsterVault is designed to be achievable by a small development effort assisted by strong tooling and automation.

Product implications:

- content should be data-driven where practical;
- systems should generate combinatorial depth from reusable mechanics;
- art/content scope should prioritize recognizability over bespoke complexity;
- high-maintenance features should prove clear retention/social/commercial value before acceptance;
- launch scope should be determined by polish and product completeness rather than raw content count.

## 9. Commercial Priority

The commercial objective is not maximum short-term Robux extraction.

The priority sequence is:

> **fun core loop -> retention -> social value -> organic discovery -> sustainable monetization -> scale**

A monetization feature that improves short-term revenue while materially weakening earlier stages violates the GDS-1 commercial strategy.
