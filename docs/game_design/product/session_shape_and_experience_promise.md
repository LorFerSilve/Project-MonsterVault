# Session Shape and Experience Promise

> **Status:** Design Complete  
> **Owning phase:** GDS-1  
> **Authority:** Product-level first-session and repeat-session pacing expectations

## 1. Session Philosophy

MonsterVault must support both quick Roblox sessions and longer collection/event sessions without making either feel invalid.

The target **normal session** is approximately **10–25 minutes**, while the product should still offer meaningful progress in a **3–5 minute** short session and enough optional goals to support **30–60 minute** extended sessions.

This is a product pacing target, not a forced timer.

## 2. Time-to-Fun Contract

A first-time player should reach meaningful gameplay with minimal delay.

Product targets:

- interactive control: effectively immediate after loading;
- a clearly visible meaningful target or next action: within roughly **30–45 seconds**;
- first real capture attempt: within roughly **60 seconds**;
- first successfully secured creature: target within **3 minutes**;
- first visible progression choice or upgrade: target within **6 minutes**.

Exact onboarding mechanics are owned by GDS-3. These targets define what that onboarding must achieve.

## 3. First-Session Shape

The intended first session follows this emotional progression:

```text
See something desirable
  -> understand how to pursue it
  -> attempt capture
  -> secure a creature
  -> bring value back to the vault
  -> see the collection/progression visibly improve
  -> make one meaningful upgrade/choice
  -> discover a clear next aspiration
```

The player should experience the product promise before encountering complex economy, trading, mutation optimization, monetization, or deep menus.

## 4. Repeat-Session Shape

A returning session should generally allow the player to choose among one or more of:

- pursue a known collection goal;
- hunt a rarer creature or variant;
- improve the vault or capture capability;
- unlock/progress toward another biome;
- join a server event;
- play with friends;
- inspect/showcase collection progress;
- later, participate in trading.

The game should present a **next desirable goal** without requiring a daily checklist to make play feel worthwhile.

## 5. Short-Session Viability

A player with only a few minutes should be able to accomplish at least one meaningful action such as:

- complete a capture attempt;
- secure/return a creature;
- make a progression purchase;
- participate in a brief event segment;
- review or reorganize a collection goal.

The product must not make short sessions feel like wasted logins solely because a long timer has not completed.

## 6. Extended-Session Viability

Longer sessions should emerge from voluntary goals rather than artificial waiting.

Extended play may be supported by:

- chained exploration/capture loops;
- hunting for rare variants;
- server events;
- friend co-play;
- collection completion;
- vault progression;
- later trading and market activity.

## 7. Menu and Tutorial Boundary

The product must not open with a long tutorial, lore dump, multi-screen claim sequence, or store-first flow.

Before the player completes the first meaningful gameplay loop, menus should expose only what is required to:

- understand the next action;
- perform the action;
- recognize the reward;
- understand the next goal.

Detailed UX is owned by GDS-3/GDS-14.

## 8. Return Motivation

MonsterVault's return motivation should come primarily from:

- unfinished collection goals;
- visible progression possibilities;
- rare variants;
- new/rotating opportunities;
- social/server moments;
- future content.

Daily rewards, streaks, offline production, quests, passes, or other retention systems may exist only if later owning phases accept them. They are not prerequisites for the product to feel worth returning to.

## 9. Session-End Principle

A healthy session may end because the player chooses to stop after making meaningful progress.

The product should avoid making the only satisfying stopping point depend on:

- exhausting energy;
- paying to continue;
- losing progress if the player leaves;
- completing a long mandatory checklist;
- waiting for an arbitrary timer.

## 10. Experience Promise

At the product level, each session should deliver at least one of three satisfactions:

1. **I found or captured something I care about.**
2. **My vault/collection/progression visibly improved.**
3. **Something memorable happened with other players.**

A session that repeatedly delivers none of these is a product-design failure even if it generates playtime.
